Radios when phones and internet are down, in France (checked against French law, Arcep and CEPT, 21 Nov 2026)

1. Listen first. In a major emergency the authorities ask everyone to tune to Radio France (France Inter, franceinfo, or the local "ici" station, formerly France Bleu: "ici Paris Île-de-France") or France Télévisions. By law these broadcast official safety instructions free, unchanged and as often as needed.
2. Phone alerts need the phone network. FR-Alert messages arrive through mobile phone masts. If the network is down, a radio is how you will hear the authorities.
3. Which radio. The government kit list says: a battery radio, with spare batteries. Choose one with FM and DAB+ (Paris has DAB+; you pick the station by its name). Radio France stopped long wave (162 kHz) and medium wave in 2016, so AM is not needed for French news. Write your local FM frequencies on the printed emergency card while you can still look them up.
4. Sirens. Tested at noon on the first Wednesday of each month. A real alert is a rising and falling sound, three times 1 minute 41 seconds with 5-second gaps. The all-clear is one steady 30-second sound. When you hear an alert, go indoors and turn on the radio.
5. Family walkie-talkies (PMR446). Anyone may use them in France: no licence, no fee.
   - The rules: 446.0 to 446.2 MHz, at most 0.5 W, built-in antenna, handheld sets only (no base station or relay).
   - Newer sets have 16 channels, older ones only 8. With mixed sets, agree on one of channels 1 to 8 and set the same code on every set.
   - Range: official planning figures for reliable contact are about 100 metres between buildings in town and about 1 km in open country. You may get more; don't count on it.
   - Anyone nearby can listen, so never say anything private.
   - A handheld with a screw-off antenna or more power is not PMR446 and is not licence-free.
6. CB radio (27 MHz). Also licence-free in France, with nothing to register.
   - 40 channels, at most 4 W, voice only. Power amplifiers are illegal.
   - With a car or roof antenna it reaches further than walkie-talkies.
   - Since the 1980s CB clubs have used channel 9 for emergency calls. This is a custom, not an official service: nobody is guaranteed to be listening.
7. Amateur ("ham") radio needs an exam run by ANFR and a personal call sign. Transmitting on ham frequencies without both is an offence.
   - In an emergency or disaster, a licensed ham may pass messages for people who are not hams.
   - Volunteer ham groups (ADRASEC) are approved by the State to help the rescue services with communications. Paris region: ADRASEC 75-92-93-94, 77, 78, 91 and 95.
8. Real distress. The international radio rules (ITU Radio Regulations, No. 4.9) say nothing in them stops a station in distress from using any radio to get help. No French text applying this to private people was found. Use PMR446 and CB first; use any other transmitter only if a life is at risk.
9. For antennas you can make from wire, and for powering radios, see the US Army radio manual FM 24-18 (in arc/guides/maps-radio).

Sources (checked 21 Nov 2026):
- Code de la sécurité intérieure, R732-25 and R732-28 (full text read on Légifrance, 21 Nov 2026); Assemblée nationale written question 14238 (answer, 12 Mar 2024): alerts through Radio France and France Télévisions, the broadcaster agreements still in force, the monthly siren test.
- FR-Alert official site (fr-alert.gouv.fr, Ministry of the Interior), FAQ: cell broadcast through the phone masts, received only by phones connected to the network (read 21 Nov 2026). Service-public.fr, FR-Alert (2 Nov 2022).
- SGDSN, "Tous responsables" (20 Nov 2025): battery radio with spare batteries; the siren signal and the all-clear.
- Assemblée nationale written question 1477 (answer, 6 Mar 2018) and ANFR press release (20 Dec 2016): Radio France off medium wave from January 2016 and long wave from end of December 2016.
- Arcom, "How to receive the radio", and its 2021 report on Radio France: DAB+ in Paris since 2014.
- Arcep decision 2014-1263 as amended by 2023-1412 (annex read on Légifrance, 21 Nov 2026; no later amendment listed): PMR446 in France, 446.0-446.2 MHz, 500 mW, integral antennas only, no base or relay stations. (A 2026 CEPT decision allows 1 W from 2027; France had not adopted it on 21 Nov 2026.)
- CEPT ECC Decision (15)05 (amended 30 Oct 2026) and ECC Report 369 (Jan 2026): PMR446 without individual permission, channels, planning ranges (0.095 km urban, 0.965 km rural at 500 mW).
- Arrêté du 31 mars 1992 (CB sets, in force 21 Nov 2026 on Légifrance): 40 channels, 26.965 to 27.405 MHz, 4 W (art. 4), amplifiers banned (art. 6), voice only (art. 7). Assemblée nationale written question 26326 (answer, 21 Jul 2020): no declaration, authorisation or fee for CB.
- Assemblée nationale written question 113975 (19 Dec 2006): channel 9 used by CB associations for urgent calls since the early 1980s.
- Arcep decision 2012-1241 and ANFR, "Les certificats": ham operator certificate and call sign; messages for third parties only in emergencies or disaster relief.
- Arrêté du 9 janvier 2025 (JORF 14 Jan 2025, read on Légifrance 21 Nov 2026): national civil-security approval of FNRASEC and the listed ADRASEC groups, for communications in rescue operations, for three years from 3 Jan 2025.
- ITU Radio Regulations (2024), Vol. 1, No. 4.9: the distress clause.
